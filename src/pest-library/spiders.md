summary: "Most spiders found in Ontario homes are harmless and actually help control other insects — but webs, egg sacs, and occasional bites make them an unwelcome guest."
appearance: "Household spiders in Northern Ontario vary widely in size and colour, from small cellar spiders with long, thin legs to larger wolf spiders that hunt on the ground rather than building webs. Most are brown, grey, or black."
habitat: "Web-building species set up in quiet, undisturbed corners — basements, garages, window corners, and storage areas — while hunting species like wolf spiders wander in search of prey and may be found on floors or in damp areas. Spiders are typically drawn indoors by the presence of other insects and by shelter during cooler months."
risks:
  - "Most local species are harmless to people, though bites can occasionally cause irritation."
  - "Webs and egg sacs are a nuisance and can make a space look neglected."
  - "A noticeable spider population often signals a larger population of other insects that spiders are feeding on."
preventionTips:
  - "Reduce outdoor lighting near entrances, or switch to warm-toned bulbs, to cut down on insects that attract spiders."
  - "Vacuum webs and egg sacs regularly, particularly in basements and garages."
  - "Seal gaps around windows, doors, and utility lines."
  - "Reduce clutter in storage areas to eliminate hiding spots."
