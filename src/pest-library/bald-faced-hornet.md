summary: "A black-and-white wasp (not a true hornet) known for building large, football-shaped paper nests high in trees, on structures, or under eaves."
appearance: "Despite the common name, the bald-faced hornet is actually a type of wasp, related to yellowjackets. It's mostly black with distinctive white or ivory markings on the face and the tip of the abdomen, and is noticeably larger than a typical yellowjacket."
habitat: "Nests are large, greyish, football- or teardrop-shaped, and enclosed in a papery envelope, often built high in trees, on the sides of buildings, or under roof eaves. A mature nest can reach the size of a basketball or larger by late summer and house several hundred workers."
risks:
  - "They are highly defensive of their nest and will sting repeatedly if it's disturbed."
  - "Large nests built close to entrances, walkways, or windows pose an ongoing risk."
  - "Nests high on structures or in trees can be difficult and dangerous to remove without proper equipment."
preventionTips:
  - "Inspect trees, eaves, and the upper areas of structures periodically during summer."
  - "Keep a safe distance from any nest you spot and avoid vibrations or loud disturbances nearby."
  - "Trim tree branches away from the house to reduce likely nesting sites close to the building."
  - "Contact a professional for removal — these nests are large and require the right equipment and timing."
