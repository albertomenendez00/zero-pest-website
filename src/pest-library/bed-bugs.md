summary: "Small, flat, reddish-brown insects that feed on blood at night and hide in seams, cracks, and crevices near where people sleep."
appearance: "Adult bed bugs are oval, flat, and reddish-brown, about 4–5 mm long — roughly the size of an apple seed. After feeding, their bodies swell and darken. Eggs are tiny, whitish, and often found in clusters within cracks and seams."
habitat: "Bed bugs hide close to where people rest for extended periods — mattress seams, box springs, headboards, bed frames, and nearby furniture. In heavier infestations they can spread to baseboards, outlets, picture frames, and wall-to-wall carpeting edges. They don't fly or jump, but travel readily in luggage, clothing, and used furniture."
risks:
  - "Bites cause itching and discomfort and can disrupt sleep."
  - "Infestations spread quickly between rooms in multi-unit buildings if not addressed."
  - "They are resilient to many DIY treatments and can persist for months without a proper program."
preventionTips:
  - "Inspect secondhand furniture and mattresses carefully before bringing them inside."
  - "Use protective, bed-bug-rated covers on mattresses and box springs."
  - "Wash and heat-dry clothing and linens after travel."
  - "Reduce clutter around beds and sofas to limit hiding spots."
