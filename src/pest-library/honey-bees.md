habitat: "Wild honey bee colonies build wax comb in sheltered cavities — hollow trees, wall voids, chimneys, or sheds — and can house tens of thousands of bees. Colonies are typically long-term and highly organized, unlike wasp colonies that die off each winter."
risks:
  - "Stings can cause pain and, in people with allergies, a serious reaction requiring medical attention."
  - "A large colony established inside a wall void can, over time, cause honey and wax damage to the structure."
  - "Because honey bees are vital pollinators, indiscriminate extermination is discouraged and, in some cases, colonies are best relocated rather than destroyed."
preventionTips:
  - "Seal potential entry points into wall voids, chimneys, and sheds before swarm season in spring."
  - "If you notice a small swarm, avoid disturbing it — swarms are often temporarily resting and may move on naturally within a day or two."
  - "For an established colony inside a structure, ask about live removal or relocation options rather than extermination."
