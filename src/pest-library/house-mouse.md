commonName: "House Mouse"
summary: "Small, greyish-brown rodents that can squeeze through openings the size of a dime and are the most common rodent found in Ontario homes."
appearance: "House mice are small, typically 6–10 cm in body length with a similarly long, thin, mostly hairless tail. Fur is usually greyish-brown, with a lighter underside. Droppings are small, dark, and rod-shaped — often the first sign of activity."
habitat: "Mice nest in warm, hidden locations close to food — wall voids, insulation, cluttered storage areas, and spaces behind appliances. As temperatures drop in fall, mice actively seek shelter indoors, entering through remarkably small gaps around foundations, utility lines, and door thresholds."
risks:
  - "Constant gnawing can damage wiring, insulation, and stored belongings, and in some cases creates a fire risk."
  - "Droppings and urine can contaminate food preparation areas and trigger allergies."
  - "Mice breed quickly, so a small early sign of activity can become a larger infestation within weeks."
preventionTips:
  - "Seal gaps around foundations, utility lines, and doors — mice can fit through openings as small as 6 mm."
  - "Store dry food, including pet food, in airtight containers."
  - "Reduce clutter in basements, garages, and storage areas to eliminate nesting sites."
  - "Keep an eye out for droppings, gnaw marks, or scratching sounds, especially in fall and winter."
