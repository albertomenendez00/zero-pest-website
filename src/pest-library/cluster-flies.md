commonName: "Cluster Flies"
summary: "Slow-moving, dark grey flies that gather in large numbers on sun-warmed walls in fall, seeking shelter indoors for the winter."
appearance: "Cluster flies are slightly larger and slower-moving than a typical house fly, dark grey with golden hairs on the thorax, and overlapping wings when at rest. They're most noticeable in fall, when they gather in large groups ('clusters') on sun-exposed exterior walls."
habitat: "In late summer and fall, cluster flies seek out sheltered spaces to overwinter, entering attics and wall voids in large numbers, often through small gaps around soffits, fascia boards, and rooflines. They spend the winter dormant, then re-emerge on warm, sunny days in late winter and spring, sometimes appearing inside living spaces in surprising numbers."
risks:
  - "Large numbers can accumulate inside wall voids and attics, become disoriented, and emerge indoors on warm days."
  - "Dead flies that accumulate in wall voids or light fixtures can attract secondary pests."
  - "Their sheer numbers can be an unpleasant surprise for homeowners unfamiliar with the seasonal pattern."
preventionTips:
  - "Seal gaps at soffits, fascia boards, attic vents, and rooflines before fall."
  - "Repair damaged window screens and weatherstripping."
  - "Vacuum flies as they appear indoors rather than crushing them, and empty the canister outside."
  - "Consider an exterior perimeter treatment in late summer, before flies seek entry for the winter."
