commonName: "Carpenter Ants"
summary: "Large black (or black and red) ants that excavate galleries in damp or decayed wood — one of the few household pests that can cause real structural damage."
appearance: "Carpenter ants are among the largest ants found in Ontario homes, ranging from 6–13 mm. They have a single node between the thorax and abdomen, an evenly rounded thorax (in profile), and elbowed antennae. Most are solid black, though some species have reddish colouring on the thorax."
habitat: "Unlike termites, carpenter ants don't eat wood — they excavate it to build smooth, gallery-like nests, usually in wood that is damp, decayed, or has been previously water-damaged. Common sites include window and door frames, roof eaves, deck posts, and wall voids near plumbing leaks. Satellite nests may form indoors while the main colony remains outdoors in a tree stump or woodpile."
risks:
  - "Prolonged activity can weaken structural wood, especially around leaks or poor drainage."
  - "Multiple satellite nests make DIY treatment difficult, since spraying visible ants rarely reaches the main colony."
  - "Activity is often a sign of an underlying moisture problem that needs to be addressed."
preventionTips:
  - "Fix leaks and improve drainage around the foundation, roofline, and windows."
  - "Keep firewood, mulch, and wood debris away from the house and off the ground."
  - "Trim tree branches and shrubs so they don't touch the roof or siding."
  - "Watch for coarse, sawdust-like shavings near baseboards, sills, or beams — an early warning sign."
