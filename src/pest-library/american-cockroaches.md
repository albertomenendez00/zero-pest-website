summary: "One of the largest common cockroach species, reddish-brown with a pale marking behind the head, often associated with drains, basements, and sewers."
appearance: "American cockroaches are large, typically 30–50 mm long, reddish-brown, with a faint yellowish figure-eight marking on the area behind the head. Both males and females have wings that cover most of the abdomen, and they are capable of short, gliding flights in warm conditions."
habitat: "They prefer warm, humid environments and are commonly associated with basements, floor drains, sewers, and utility tunnels. In commercial buildings they often travel between units through shared plumbing and drain lines. Outdoors, they can be found in leaf litter, mulch, and woodpiles before moving indoors with cooling temperatures."
risks:
  - "Their size and speed make sightings especially alarming to occupants and customers."
  - "Association with drains and sewers raises hygiene concerns for food-service and multi-unit properties."
  - "Populations can move between connected units in apartment and commercial buildings."
preventionTips:
  - "Keep drain covers in place and clean drains regularly to remove organic buildup."
  - "Seal gaps around pipes, floor drains, and foundation penetrations."
  - "Reduce moisture in basements and crawlspaces with proper drainage or a dehumidifier."
  - "Address activity promptly, since drain-associated infestations often require professional access to shared plumbing."
