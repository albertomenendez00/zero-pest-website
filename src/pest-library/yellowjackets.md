commonName: "Yellowjackets"
summary: "Aggressive, boldly striped black-and-yellow wasps that build enclosed paper nests, often in the ground or wall voids, and defend them vigorously."
appearance: "Yellowjackets are shorter and stockier than paper wasps, with bold black-and-yellow banding and a fast, darting flight pattern. They are especially attracted to sugary foods and proteins in late summer, when colonies are at their largest and most defensive."
habitat: "Nests are enclosed in a papery outer envelope and are commonly built underground in old rodent burrows, inside wall voids, under decks, or in dense shrubs. A single nest can house thousands of workers by late summer, and ground nests are often not noticed until they are accidentally disturbed."
risks:
  - "They are more aggressive than paper wasps and will defend nests vigorously if disturbed, including underground nests that are easy to step on unknowingly."
  - "Large late-summer colonies increase the chance of multiple stings."
  - "Nests hidden in walls or underground are difficult to locate and treat safely without professional equipment."
preventionTips:
  - "Keep outdoor eating areas clean and cover food and sugary drinks."
  - "Seal gaps at soffits, siding, and foundation vents that could allow access to wall voids."
  - "Be cautious around holes in the ground or mulch beds in late summer, especially near increased wasp activity."
  - "Never attempt to treat a ground or wall-void nest yourself — contact a licensed technician."
